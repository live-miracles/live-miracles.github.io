vMix describes audio on several different scales, and the numbers in its API are not the ones on the faders. This page explains them, because the thresholds of the Translator and AutoGain scripts are written in them.

The scales

Scale Range Where it is used
Volume 0 to 100 The volume bars in the interface, and SetVolumeFade
Amplitude 0 to 1 The level of an audio bus or an input's meter in the API, meterF1 and meterF2
Amplitude2 0 to 100 The volume of an input in the API, volume
Decibels minus infinity to 0 Audio engineering. 0 dB FS is the loudest signal that can be represented

Converting between them

Amplitude  = Amplitude2 / 100
Amplitude2 = Amplitude * 100
Volume     = (Amplitude ^ 0.25) * 100
Amplitude  = (Volume / 100) ^ 4
Decibels   = 20 * Math.Log10(Amplitude)
Amplitude  = 10 ^ (Decibels / 20)

The volume scale is not linear. It is the amplitude raised to the power of a quarter, which is why the middle of a fader is much quieter than half the loudness.

Volumes in decibels

The volume bar can also be described in decibels, as the change it makes to the signal: Decibels = 20 * Math.Log10(Amplitude), which together with the conversions above gives

Volume   = 100 * 10 ^ (Decibels / 80)
Decibels = 80 * Math.Log10(Volume / 100)

The Translator settings use this, because a decibel is the same change of loudness anywhere on the scale, and a step of 1 on the volume scale is not.

What to keep in mind

  • A meter at 100 % is 0 dB FS, the loudest possible signal. A volume bar at 100 % is the full incoming signal, which for a normal voice is about the target loudness of -18 dB FS.
  • Lowering a volume bar to 50 % therefore reduces the incoming signal by 24 dB, relative to the signal, and not to -24 dB.

Thresholds in the scripts

Setting Value Equivalent
Translator VOICE_THRESHOLD_DB -20 dB 0.1 amplitude
AutoGain VOICE_THRESHOLD_DB -20 dB 0.1 amplitude
AutoGain PEAK_LOW_DB -6 dB 0.5 amplitude
AutoGain PEAK_HIGH_DB -5 dB about 0.56 amplitude
AutoGain CLIP_THRESHOLD_DB -1 dB about 0.9 amplitude

To pick your own threshold, take the level you want in decibels and use Amplitude = 10 ^ (Decibels / 20).
